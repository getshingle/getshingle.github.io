For many small companies, a static site is faster, safer and cheaper to run. tag: Technology author: david-osei cover_alt: Stacked layers representing a website built into simple files ---

A content management system, or CMS, lets your team log in and edit the website. WordPress is the best known. For many sites it is the right choice, but it comes with a cost: updates, plugins, security fixes and hosting that has to run a database.

A static site is different. It is built once into plain files and served as they are. There is nothing to log in to on the live site, and nothing to hack.

A static site is a good fit when

  • The site changes a few times a month, not every hour
  • One or two people update it, and they are comfortable with a simple editor
  • Speed and security matter more than a large plugin catalogue
  • You want low, predictable hosting costs

A CMS is a better fit when

  • Many people publish every day, with roles and approval steps
  • You need features such as user accounts, a shop with a large catalogue or member areas
  • Your team already knows a CMS well and is happy with it

What we usually recommend

For company websites with fewer than a few hundred pages, we often build a static site and add a simple editor on top. Your team gets an editing screen they understand; visitors get a site that loads instantly; and nobody has to install security updates on a Friday evening.
