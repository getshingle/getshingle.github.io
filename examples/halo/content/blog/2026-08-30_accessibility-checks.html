--- title: Five accessibility checks you can do in ten minutes summary: You do not need special software to find the most common problems. A keyboard and a few minutes are enough. tag: Accessibility author: lena-moreau ---

Accessibility audits can take days, but the most common problems show up in minutes. Try these five checks on your own website.

1. Use only the keyboard

Put your mouse away and press Tab to move through the page. Can you see where you are at every step? Can you open the menu and reach every link?

2. Zoom to 200%

Press Ctrl and + (or Cmd and + on a Mac) until the page is twice as large. Nothing should overlap, and you should not need to scroll sideways.

3. Read the link texts alone

Links that say “click here” or “read more” mean nothing out of context. Screen reader users often jump from link to link, so each link should say where it goes.

4. Check the contrast

Light grey text on a white background is hard to read for many people, especially outdoors on a phone. If you have to squint, so do your visitors.

5. Look at the pictures without the pictures

Every picture that carries information needs a short text description, called alt text. Decorative pictures do not.

If any of these checks fail, your site is probably harder to use than it should be. Our accessibility audits go much deeper, but these five are a good start.
