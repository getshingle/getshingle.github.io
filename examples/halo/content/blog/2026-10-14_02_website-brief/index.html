When three agencies quote very different prices for the same website, it usually means they read three different projects into the same brief. A good brief makes that much less likely, and it does not need to be long.

1. Why you need a new website

Start with the problem, not the solution. “Customers call us to ask our opening hours” is more useful than “we need a modern design”.

2. Who it is for

Describe the two or three groups of people who visit the site and what each of them wants to do there.

3. What it must do

List the features that are essential, and separately the ones that would be nice to have. Agencies can then price both and you can choose.

4. What already exists

Link to your current site, your logo and brand guidelines, and any content you want to keep. Say who will write new content.

5. Time and budget

Give a launch date and a budget range. A range is not a commitment; it tells agencies whether to propose a simple site or an ambitious one.

6. Who decides

Name the person who will approve the work, and how much time they have for the project each week.

That is the whole brief. Send it to two or three studios, ask each one what they would do first, and compare the answers as much as the prices. You can also send it to us.
